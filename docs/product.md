# Covenant product brief

Oct 31, 2026 · @Teja

Covenant is a digital-detox launcher. It makes your phone calm by default and keeps the promise you made when you were calm. This brief says who it's for and what it does; [spec.md](spec.md) turns it into rules and screens.

## Who it's for

**Reclaimers.** Adults aged 18–30 in India, on Android, who lose three hours or more a day to feeds: Reels, Shorts, Instagram, YouTube, X and games.

- They've tried Digital Wellbeing timers and skipped them with one tap.
- They can't switch to a dumbphone, because UPI, OTPs, maps, cabs and work chat live on the phone.
- They want their evenings, sleep and focus back, and they'll make a firm promise if setup is quick and it never blocks a payment or a call.
- They aren't developers. They install from the Play Store and set up without a computer.

Covenant is not for children or parental control, employers, people who'd be better off with an actual dumbphone, or blocking content inside an app (Reels inside Instagram).

## The promise

Your phone is calm by default.

- Calls, payments, maps and messages always work.
- Feeds open only in the windows you planned, for the minutes you allowed.
- Tightening your plan is instant. Loosening it waits 72 hours.

## A day with Covenant

1. You pick up the phone. Home shows the current block and your essentials, and nothing else.
2. You reach for a feed outside its window. It isn't on home or in search. A notification or a link leads to a calm screen that says when it opens. If you truly need it now, you use your pass: one a week.
3. Inside its window, a three-second ink bloom shows the minutes left, then asks Open or Let go. Letting go counts.
4. Feed notifications wait and arrive as one summary when their window opens. Night is essentials only, in grayscale.
5. Today's page says it in one line: "Feeds today: 35 min (before Covenant: 3 h 10 min). Let go 4 times."

## Principles

- **Calm by default.** Home shows only what you need now.
- **Decide once, when calm.** Plans are made ahead, and a looser plan waits out the cooldown.
- **Exits are rare and visible.** One pass a week, never a daily habit.
- **Never in the way of life.** Calls, payments, maps, OTPs and alarms always work.
- **Every reach is a chance to let go.** A short pause and a choice before tempting apps.
- **Show change, not dashboards.** Before and now, in one line. No streaks and no shame.
- **Five-minute setup, no computer.**

## Eight ideas

| Idea | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Essentials | Apps that open in every block and sit on home: phone, messages, maps, camera, UPI, authenticator |
| Tempting apps | The apps that pull you in. Only these are held to blocks |
| Tools | Every other app. Opens any time from search, but stays off home |
| Blocks | The day split into named windows; each tempting app opens only in the ones you gave it |
| Time limits | Minutes a tempting app gets inside a block |
| Pass | Opens one outside app for 10 minutes after a 1-minute wait. One a week |
| Quiet | Essentials only, now: for the rest of the block or 30, 60 or 90 minutes |
| Held | Notifications from outside apps, delivered as one summary when their block starts |
| The promise | The seal, a first calibration week when every change is instant, and the 72-hour cooldown after it |

## How we'll know it works

After two weeks with Covenant as your only launcher:

- Time in feeds is half the baseline from the week before install, or less.
- At most one pass a week is used.
- The pause gets a Let go a meaningful share of the time.
- You'd keep it as your launcher.

## Distribution

The Play Store first, because Play Protect in India blocks accessibility apps installed from a browser. Play allows them with an accessibility declaration, an in-app disclosure and consent. GitHub Releases, Obtainium and F-Droid follow for people who prefer them.
